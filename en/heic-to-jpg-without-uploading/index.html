Why does this matter?

Every time a photo leaves your device to be "converted," it sits on someone else's server for at least a moment. For most people that's fine — but if the photos are personal, or you just don't like handing files to a stranger's server, it's better if the conversion never leaves your phone or computer at all.

How it works

Your browser reads the HEIC file directly and re-encodes it as JPG using code that runs locally — the same way a photo-editing app on your phone would. No file is sent anywhere. You can even turn off your Wi-Fi after the page loads and it will still work.

Common questions

Does this work on iPhone itself, or only on a computer?It works in any modern browser — iPhone, Android, Windows, or Mac. On iPhone it's mostly useful when you're sending photos to someone whose device can't open HEIC.
Is there a file size or quantity limit?You can convert several photos at once. Since everything runs on your device, the real limit is your device's own memory, not a server quota.
Does it keep the original photo quality?Yes — it's a straight re-encode, not a re-compress-and-strip. EXIF orientation is preserved so photos don't come out sideways.
